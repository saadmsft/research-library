## Bottom line

::: verified What I could check
In the works attributed to Sheikh Abdul Qadir Jilani that I read, ==there is no breath technique==. Not in *Futuh al-Ghayb*, *al-Fath ar-Rabbani*, *al-Ghunya* or the *Malfuzat*, and not in *Sirr al-Asrar*, whose attribution is itself disputed. What he does teach is *vigilance of every breath* in the company of Allah, an attitude and not a method.
:::

The breath technique appears in the Qadiri line later and in a documented way. Dara Shikoh, writing in 1646, describes habs-i nafas and says it descends from Jilani. That is a claim made nearly five centuries after Jilani's death, and it should be read as the line's own tradition, not as evidence from his texts.

This paper is a continuation of [Pas Anfas: guarding the breath](@sufi-practice/pas-anfas), which sets out the terms.

## Who he was

::: timeline Dates, with the disputes marked
c. 1077 | Born | Details of birth are disputed (Chabbi)
about 25 years | Retreat before public preaching |
1127 | Begins preaching in Baghdad | Founds the Madrasa al-Qadiriyya
1166 | Dies | A Hanbali jurist and preacher
:::

He was a Hanbali jurist and preacher in Baghdad. The title *Ghawth al-Aʿẓam* is a later hagiographic one. Lawrence thinks the claim of sayyid descent may have been constructed after the fact.[^dates] None of this lowers his standing in the tradition. It does mean that careful work separates what he is recorded as saying from what the line later attached to him.

## What the works actually say about breath

::: matrix Breath in the Jilani corpus
| Work | Breath technique | What is there instead |
|---|---|---|
| *Futuh al-Ghayb* | no | Discourses on conduct and the inner life; no breath instruction found |
| *Al-Fath ar-Rabbani* | no | "Breaths" of the siddiqin means spiritual influence, not technique |
| *Al-Ghunya* | no | Breath as vigilance in murāqaba, and as a divine name |
| *Sirr al-Asrar* | no: the talqin turns the face, not the breath | Staged inner dhikr; attribution disputed |
| *Malfuzat* | no | Recorded sayings; no breath instruction found |
| Dara Shikoh, *Risala-i Haqq-numa* (1646) | yes: habs-i nafas | Attributes the practice to Jilani as "Awurd-burd" |
:::

In Holland's translation of *al-Ghunya*, four places where breath appears are worth quoting. They are the nearest the book comes to the subject.

- In the murāqaba (watchfulness) section: the seeker is "taking care of every breath he breathes in the company of Allah". That is vigilance.
- Dhikr is kept "under his breath" among fellow paupers. That means quietly, not a pranayama.
- "Praise is for the very breaths we breathe, while thankfulness is for the blessings of the senses."
- One of the divine names is *Muhsi'l-anfas*, the Enumerator of breaths.

In *al-Fath ar-Rabbani*, the "breaths" of the siddiqin are their spiritual influence on the listener.

## Sirr al-Asrar: useful, but with a caveat

*Sirr al-Asrar* (in Bayrak's translation, *The Secret of Secrets*) has the most explicit dhikr instruction of any text attributed to him. Its authenticity is ==disputed==. That should be said whenever it is cited.

What it describes, and where:

- Chapter 5, the talqin: the face is turned to the right on "La ilaha" and to the left, toward the heart, on "illa Llah", three times, from a living teacher.
- Chapter 7: dhikr aloud, then silent, descending through tongue, heart, ruh, sirr, khafi and akhfa, and ending in fana.
- Chapter 8: begin with wudu, and begin aloud.
- Chapter 21: inner, silent dhikr.

::: chain Stages of dhikr in Sirr al-Asrar, ch. 7 (attribution disputed)
Tongue | Dhikr aloud, the beginning | disputed
Heart | Silent dhikr moves inward | disputed
Ruh | The spirit | disputed
Sirr | The secret | disputed
Khafi | The hidden | disputed
Akhfa | The most hidden | disputed
Fana | Passing away; the end of the descent | disputed
:::

Nothing in these chapters is a breath instruction. The turning of the face on the kalima is the nearest thing, and it moves the head, not the lungs.

::: discarded A popular summary I rejected
An AI-generated summary of *Sirr al-Asrar* described a method of "breathing to the heart", focusing on the navel, and a forty-day course, and attached a quotation to it. I could not find any of this in the text. I discarded it and none of it is used here.
:::

## How breath practice enters the Qadiri line

::: breath Dara Shikoh's habs-i nafas, as he reports it
Habs-i nafas | in:la ilaha | hold:retained | out:illa'llah
:::

Dara Shikoh's *Risala-i Haqq-numa* (1646; Vasu's translation) describes the practice as above, with the in-breath through the left nostril and the out-breath through the right.[^dara] He says it comes down from Ghaus-us-Saqalain, that is, Jilani, as "Awurd-burd". He adds that Mian Mir introduced mental recitation during the retention. He also warns of suffocation and lung injury, and he describes an inner sound called Sultan-ul-Azkar.

::: unverified Hagiography, kept for context
Stories in the same literature say Mulla Shah held his breath overnight for fifteen years and did not sleep for thirty. I treat these as hagiography. They are not evidence about what is physically possible, and I would not repeat them as fact.
:::

The other route into the line is the Qalandariyya-Qadiriyya pas-i anfas of Shah Wahhaj al-Din Qalandar, which is natural-breath "Allah" in, "Hu" out, with no retention.

## Sultan Bahu

## A safer way to read this

::: caution My reasoning, not a source
If you are approaching Qadiri dhikr, the textual record supports vigilance of the breath and a quiet inner dhikr, under a teacher. It does not support forcing the breath. Forced breathing and retention carry risks of dizziness, anxiety and fainting, and need more care with cardiac, respiratory, seizure or pregnancy conditions. The natural-breath form is the low-risk form. This is general reasoning, not medical advice.
:::
